Vastu & Modern Design

Ancient principles. Contemporary spaces.

Vastu Shastra is a traditional framework for how a built space relates to direction, light, airflow and the people who use it. At Devagya, we treat it as exactly that — a framework to be studied alongside, not instead of, the practical realities of a site.

We don't present Vastu as a guarantee of wealth, health or specific life outcomes, and we won't tell you it is. What we offer is a considered integration of classical spatial principles with real, measurable factors — sun-path, structure, airflow, proportion — so that the guidance you receive is something you can actually build from.

Where a traditional principle and a practical constraint pull in different directions, we say so, and work through it with you rather than picking one and staying quiet about the other.

Identity First

Identity first. Layout second.

Before a single wall is drawn, we ask what a space is meant to feel like, who moves through it, and what it needs to do — for the family that lives there, or the company that works from it.

Vastu principles and structural planning are then applied to that answer, not the other way around. The proportion, the light, the sequence of rooms — all of it follows from the identity of the space, not a template.

Applies to a private home, a company's workspace, or a full building — single floors and multi-unit projects included.

About

Three disciplines.
One practice.

For generations, Vastu Shastra has guided how a home should meet the sun, how a threshold should be placed, how a room should hold the people living in it. Every project at Devagya starts there.

We test that reading against measurable data: sun-path and daylight study, soil and drainage behaviour, wind and ventilation, structural load. Where tradition and measurement agree, we build with confidence. Where they don't, we work out why before a single wall goes up.

Only then do we design the building itself — structure, material, proportion, light — the way any serious architecture practice would, for a client building in the present.

We work with individuals building their first home, companies planning their next office, and developers laying out a floor or an entire building. The scale changes. The method doesn't.
