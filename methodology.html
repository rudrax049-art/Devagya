Methodology

Ancient principles. Contemporary understanding.

We don't treat traditional Vastu and modern design as competing systems. Here's exactly how the three pillars work, and — just as importantly — what we don't claim.

1. Vastu Shastra

Vastu Shastra is a traditional Indian framework for how a built space relates to direction, entry, and the placement of rooms and elements. We study it as a classical discipline in its own right — not as something that needs modern science to validate it, and not as something we present as scientifically proven.

It gives us a starting lens: where an entrance might sit, how a plot's directions relate to different functions, which placements classical texts associate with which uses.

2. Architectural Physics

This is where we check the traditional reading against measurable, physical reality:

  • Sun-path analysis — how sunlight actually moves across your specific site through the year, not a generic assumption.
  • Cross-ventilation dynamics — how air realistically moves through a planned layout, based on openings and orientation.
  • Structural and site factors — load, soil and drainage considerations that any sound design has to account for.

On electromagnetic field (EMF) exposure: some clients ask about electrical wiring, transformers or equipment near a site. Where relevant, we can note where these exist on a plan as a practical awareness factor. We do not claim to "mitigate" EMF exposure or its health effects — that's outside what spatial design can verify, and we won't claim otherwise.

3. Environmental & Positive Psychology

This pillar is a design lens, not a set of guarantees. Research in environmental psychology discusses how aspects of a built environment can be associated with perceived comfort, privacy, attention and stress experience. We apply this cautiously:

Clarity & cognitive load

A layout that's easy to read can reduce the mental effort of simply navigating it.

Privacy & comfort

How a room is enclosed or overlooked can encourage a sense of ease or exposure.

Restoration & focus

Quieter zones, separated from high-traffic areas, can support attention.

Connection with nature

Sightlines to greenery or sky can be designed for, where a site allows it.

What we won't say: that a layout "creates happiness," "cures stress," or guarantees any psychological or health outcome. We use language like "can support" and "is associated with" deliberately, because that's what's defensible.

A note on scientific integrity.

No. We don't make that claim, and we're direct with clients who ask. Vastu is a traditional spatial framework we apply thoughtfully — not a guarantee of any life outcome.

The architectural physics (sun-path, ventilation, structure) is standard, measurable engineering. The psychology pillar draws on real research fields, described cautiously. Vastu itself is a traditional framework, not a scientific claim — we don't present it as one.
